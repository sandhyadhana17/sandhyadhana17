# Media Research Project: Bylines by Women Journalists in Newspapers – An Analysis

## 1. Project Overview and Background
This document analyzes a media research project conducted by the Department of Journalism at M.O.P. Vaishnav College for Women (Autonomous) in Chennai [cite: 1]. The study, authored by Abhirami S, Sandhya D, Sargam Bhasin, Shruthi S, and Sriya, investigates the distribution and frequency of bylines obtained by women journalists in major Indian newspapers [cite: 1]. 

The historical context provided in the project highlights that women entered Indian journalism through revolutionary writings during the Freedom Struggle, with pioneers like Vidya Munshi (the first woman journalist in India), Homai Vyarawalla (first woman photojournalist), and Pratima Puri (first television newsreader) paving the way [cite: 1]. Despite these early breakthroughs and the fact that female journalists have secured more jobs in media recently, women remain largely underrepresented in leadership roles and "hard news" reporting [cite: 1]. 

## 2. Methodology
The study employed Content Analysis to track bylines across three national English daily newspapers: *The Hindu*, *Times of India* (TOI), and *The New Indian Express* (NIE) [cite: 1].
* **Timeframe:** The analysis spans five years from 2015 to 2019 [cite: 1]. 
* **Sampling:** Three months were examined in 2015 and 2016, and two months were analyzed for the years 2017, 2018, and 2019 [cite: 1]. For each month, the first 15 days of the Chennai editions were considered [cite: 1].
* **Categorization:** Bylines were sorted into specific "beats," which were further grouped into "Hard News" (Politics, Crime, Business, Civic, International, Sports, Opinion, Health) and "Soft News" (Arts, Culture, Lifestyle, Entertainment, Science & Technology) [cite: 1]. Generic agency bylines (e.g., PTI, Reuters) were excluded [cite: 1].

## 3. Findings and Analysis (2015 - 2019)

### 2015 Findings
* **The Hindu:** Registered the highest number of female bylines (997) [cite: 1]. Women contributed notably to both hard and soft news, with 154 bylines in Politics and 113 in International news [cite: 1]. The highest beats were Arts (136) and Lifestyle (134) [cite: 1].
* **Times of India (TOI):** Recorded 512 bylines, predominantly in Entertainment (128) and Lifestyle (80) [cite: 1]. Hard news beats like Politics (44) saw negligible contributions [cite: 1].
* **The New Indian Express (NIE):** Recorded 313 bylines, largely driven by Lifestyle (82) and Entertainment (43) [cite: 1]. Law and International news had the least representation [cite: 1].

### 2016 Findings
* **The Hindu:** Saw a decrease to 650 bylines [cite: 1]. Politics (110) saw a spike during the budget month of March [cite: 1].
* **Times of India:** Jumped to 812 bylines [cite: 1]. A significant spike in Politics (43) occurred in December 2016 following the demise of former Chief Minister J. Jayalalithaa, but Lifestyle (112) and Entertainment (153) remained the highest contributors [cite: 1].
* **The New Indian Express:** Totaled 509 bylines, with a heavy reliance on agency news for hard news beats [cite: 1]. Lifestyle contributed the most (144) [cite: 1].

### 2017 Findings
* **The Hindu:** Tallied 682 bylines, with Lifestyle dominating at 160 bylines [cite: 1]. Beats like Crime (10) and Sports (27) rarely featured female bylines [cite: 1].
* **Times of India:** Totaled 454 bylines [cite: 1]. Business saw a hike (93), though articles were often authored repeatedly by a few specific journalists [cite: 1]. Entertainment remained high (116) [cite: 1].
* **The New Indian Express:** Counted 258 bylines [cite: 1]. Soft news in supplementary pages (Indulge, Edex) formed the bulk of women's contributions [cite: 1].

### 2018 Findings
* **The Hindu:** Reached 801 bylines [cite: 1]. Lifestyle (169) and Politics (96) were the primary areas of focus [cite: 1].
* **Times of India:** Dropped to 435 bylines [cite: 1]. Political bylines fell significantly to 71, while Lifestyle remained the highest at 97 [cite: 1].
* **The New Indian Express:** Tallied 456 bylines, maintaining consistency across beats but still largely dominated by male reporters [cite: 1].

### 2019 Findings
* **The Hindu:** Recorded 725 bylines [cite: 1]. Lifestyle (130) and Entertainment (85) were the top beats [cite: 1]. Notably, women gained 40 bylines in Sports, a significant figure for a traditionally male-dominated arena [cite: 1].
* **Times of India:** Amassed 763 bylines [cite: 1]. The majority were clustered in Lifestyle (125) and Entertainment (95) [cite: 1].
* **The New Indian Express:** Totaled 569 bylines [cite: 1]. Lifestyle (79) and Entertainment (76) led, while Crime (5) and Politics (21) lagged significantly [cite: 1].

## 4. Literature Review and Structural Challenges
The project also synthesized extensive literature on the global gender gap in journalism:
* **The Stagnation of Progress:** Studies like the Global Media Monitoring Project (GMMP) indicate that female representation in newsrooms globally stagnated at 37% between 2005 and 2015 [cite: 1].
* **The Hard vs. Soft News Divide:** Research consistently shows a "creative patriarchy" where male reporters are assigned hard news (politics, crime) and female reporters are relegated to soft news (lifestyle, arts, health) [cite: 1]. 
* **Leadership Gap:** While there are female reporters on staff, leadership positions remain overwhelmingly male-dominated [cite: 1]. Female editors often face double standards and resistance from subordinates, with some newsrooms historically citing "risk" as a reason to keep women off field beats [cite: 1].
* **The Byline Gap:** Reports such as "Divided 2019: The Media Gender Gap" revealed that 63% of bylines globally go to men, leaving only 37% for women [cite: 1].

## 5. Conclusion
The comprehensive analysis of the five-year data validates the core hypothesis: while the raw number of bylines attributed to women increased between 2015 and 2019, qualitative parity has not been achieved [cite: 1]. Female journalists are severely underrepresented in hard news categories—such as Politics, Crime, Civic, and Business—where their bylines actually decreased from an average of 127 in 2015 to just 79 in 2019 [cite: 1]. Women remain largely pigeonholed into writing for "soft" beats like Lifestyle, Arts, and Entertainment [cite: 1]. The study concludes that an increase in overall numbers does not directly equate to equitable representation or equal opportunity within the newsroom ecosystem [cite: 1].
